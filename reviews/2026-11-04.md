## 1. girl_finishes_your_sentences

**Line check:**
1. She finishes your sentences, and it isn't interrupting. PASS.
2. She says your punchline before you do. **FAIL.** Saying someone's punchline before them is exactly what interrupting looks like, so the line works against the hook. "Word for word" is also unclear: whose words? Replacement: "You start a sentence and she says the end of it with you, same words, same time."
3. She fixes a detail in your own story without looking up. PASS. Concrete and funny.
4. She mouths along "like she's hearing it for the first time again". **FAIL.** This contradicts itself: if she mouths along, she knows the story. Replacement: "She mouths along when you tell it at a party, and still laughs at the end."
5. Send line. PASS.
6. You stop telling her things, and she's "starving for new ones". **FAIL.** Nobody said she was bored, so "she's not bored" denies something no one claimed. The line is also about what *you* do, not a change in *her*, and "starving for new ones" leans toward fortune cookie. Replacement: "But the day she lets you get the story wrong and doesn't correct you... she's not being polite. She's stopped listening."
7. She has been listening all along. PASS.

**Hook:** Recognisable, but it's an old trope and the behaviour barely looks bad, so the surprise is weak. It's also close to the personality-trait zone ("we're in sync").
**Send:** Some men would send it. Women are less likely to recognise themselves strongly enough to send it to him.
**Twist:** As written it isn't a twist. The replacement line 6 is a real reversal in the hoodie mould.
**Risk:** None.
**Scores:** Reach 5, Engagement 5, Relatability 6. P(1k) 20%.
**Verdict:** SHIP WITH FIXES (lines 2, 4 and 6 as above). Low priority.

## Patterns across the batch

- **The line 6 template is wearing thin.** Five of nine use either "the day she stops doing X" or "if you make fun of her, she stops", and three end on advice ("ask what's wrong", "don't assume", "let him rest"). Advice endings aren't turns. Every line 6 should state what the change *means*, as the winners do ("she's saying goodbye", "it's not her feet that went cold").
- **The love-evidence beat is missing.** Winners always include one beat that proves the affection ("because it still smells like you"). Several drafts stack three beats of the annoying behaviour and never prove the love.
- **Hooks are long.** Lines 1 in scripts 3, 7 and 10 run past 2 seconds of narration.
