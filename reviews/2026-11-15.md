# Script review: 10 drafts against the 5 real scripts

I took the performance table as given and did not re-pull Metricool. This review only judges the scripts. One thing to know first: four drafts (3, 8, 9, 10) use the same turn, "But if you never once [return it]... she'll/he'll [quietly keep going or notice]". It is the weakest kind of turn in the batch. None of the real scripts uses it, and its consequence is soft every time.

---

## 1. boy_saves_you_a_seat_with_his_jacket_before_you_arrive

**1. Line check**
- L1: He saves your seat with his jacket, and it means more than a spot. OK.
- L2: He turns away three people before you arrive. OK, and strong.
- L3: At the cinema he takes the worse seat. **FAIL (drift).** This is a different behaviour (giving up the better seat), not saving one.
- L4: He does it everywhere: pub, wedding. OK, but it's a list, and after L3 it's unclear what "it" refers to.
- L5: "Stand looking for a seat next to him." **FAIL (unclear).** It reads as if you are standing next to him.
- L6: The night he doesn't save one, he has stopped making room for you. OK, and strong.
- L7: He is making space for you before you arrive. OK, but at 22 words it's long.

**2. Hook check:** Pass. The subject is general and the behaviour is concrete and visual.

**3. Beat check:** Partial. L2 and L4 are on-behaviour, but L3 wanders off it.

**4. Send check:** Pass on structure, but the wording fails (see L5).

**5. Turn check:** Pass. It names a specific future moment and reveals what it means, like hoodie's "washed and folded" turn.

**6. Closer check:** Pass, but it's about twice as long as hoodie's closer.

**7. Risk check:** None.

**8. Benchmark:** Closest to **girl_steals_hoodie** (an object stands for the bond, and the turn is "the day it stops means goodbye"). As written it **FALLS SHORT** because of L3 and L5. With the fixes it **MATCHES**: the turn is as real as hoodie's, though the closer is less tight.

**9. Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES (2 lines)**
- L3: "He keeps one hand on the jacket every time someone walks past."
- L5: "If you've never once had to look for a seat when he's there... send this to him."

## 2. boy_remembers_your_familys_birthdays_better_than_you_do

**1. Line check**
- L1: He remembers your family's birthdays better than you do. OK.
- L2: He texts your mum before you're awake. OK.
- L3: He knows your dad's date without checking. **FAIL (not a picture).** "Knows" can't be drawn, and it repeats the hook.
- L4: Your nan's birthday has its own alarm in his calendar. OK.
- L5: Send to him. OK.
- L6: "If he's the only one who ever remembers... he'll stop reminding you." **FAIL (unclear and contradictory).** The only one who remembers what? His own family's birthdays? The beats never show him reminding you. The line also punishes the hook's own premise.
- L7: He is building a life with all of you. OK, and good.

**2. Hook check:** Pass.

**3. Beat check:** Partial. L3 and L4 are nearly the same beat (he knows dates), and only L2 is an action.

**4. Send check:** Pass.

**5. Turn check:** Fail as written (see L6).

**6. Closer check:** Pass. It's quotable and reframes the trait.

**7. Risk check:** None.

**8. Benchmark:** Closest to **girl_steals_hoodie** if it takes the fixed turn below; as written it's nearest girl_no_makeup. As written it **FALLS SHORT**. With the fixes it **MATCHES** hoodie, and the closer is strong.

**9. Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES (2 lines)**
- L3: "He's already signed your mum's card before you've even bought one."
- L6: "But the year he forgets your mum's birthday... he didn't lose track. He's stopped seeing them as his family."

## 3. girl_memorizes_your_coffee_order_before_her_own

**1. Line check**
- L1: She knows your coffee order better than her own. OK.
- L2: She orders yours first, then has to think about her own. OK, and good.
- L3: She corrects the barista on your milk. OK.
- L4: She remembers your first-date order, "two sugars you don't take anymore." **FAIL (contradiction).** It says her knowledge is out of date, which undercuts "knows it better than her own."
- L5: Send to her. OK.
- L6: "If you never return the favour... she'll notice." **FAIL (no consequence).** "She'll notice" is not a cost. It's the soft reciprocity template.
- L7: "Has been paying attention the whole time." Weak. It could close any script and doesn't compress the coffee trait.

**2. Hook check:** Pass.

**3. Beat check:** Partial. L2 and L3 are good; L4 breaks.

**4. Send check:** Pass.

**5. Turn check:** Fail.

**6. Closer check:** Marginal. It stands alone, but it's generic.

**7. Risk check:** None.

**8. Benchmark:** Closest to **girl_no_makeup** (the subject is a girl, the viewer is the boy, and it sends to her). It **FALLS SHORT**: the turn is soft compared with "watch how fast the makeup goes back on", and the closer is generic compared with "feels at home with you".

**9. Verdict: CUT.** It needs L4, L6 and L7 replaced, which makes three lines and so a rewrite.

## 4. boy_walks_on_the_road_side_of_the_pavement

**1. Line check**
- L1: He takes the road side without being asked. OK, and a classic.
- L2: He switches sides the moment a car comes close. OK, and strong.
- L3: "A car park, a corridor, anywhere there's traffic." **FAIL (contradiction).** A corridor has no traffic.
- L4: If you swap sides, he steps around you to get back. OK.
- L5: Send to him. OK.
- L6: "If you walk ahead without noticing... you've never caught him doing it." **FAIL (tautology).** It says that if you don't notice, you haven't noticed. There is no consequence.
- L7: He stands between you and the world without making it a thing. OK, and the most quotable closer in the batch.

**2. Hook check:** Pass.

**3. Beat check:** Pass except L3.

**4. Send check:** Pass.

**5. Turn check:** Fail as written.

**6. Closer check:** Pass, and strong.

**7. Risk check:** Low. L4 is a small physical correction of where she walks. It reads as protective, not controlling.

**8. Benchmark:** Closest to **girl_steals_hoodie** (one repeated physical habit, with a "the day it stops" turn once fixed). As written it **FALLS SHORT** because the turn is dead. With the fixes it **MATCHES**, and the closer competes with fidelity's.

**9. Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES (2 lines)**
- L3: "He does it on an empty street at midnight, with no car in sight."
- L6: "But the day he lets you walk on the road side... he's not being lazy. He's stopped putting himself in the way."

## 5. girl_saves_you_the_last_bite_she_actually_wanted

**1. Line check**
- L1: She saves you the last bite she wanted, and she isn't "just full." OK. It's the same shape as hoodie's "not just cold."
- L2: She says she's finished, then nudges the plate toward you. OK, and excellent.
- L3: She takes the smallest dessert piece on purpose. OK.
- L4: She lets you have the last dumpling every time. OK.
- L5: If you've eaten more than your share without noticing, send it to her. OK. It's a charming confession.
- L6: If you point out she's always "full"... she'll finish her plate, and you'll miss this. OK. This is a real consequence.
- L7: She'd rather watch you enjoy it than taste it. OK, and quotable.

**2. Hook check:** Pass.

**3. Beat check:** Pass. All three beats are one-picture actions of the same behaviour, and they're the cleanest beats in the batch.

**4. Send check:** Pass.

**5. Turn check:** Pass. It mirrors girl_no_makeup's turn almost exactly. "And you'll miss this" is a slightly limp tail.

**6. Closer check:** Pass.

**7. Risk check:** Low to medium. A woman under-eating so a man can eat more is uncomfortable to some viewers and could pull "she should eat too" comments. It's not toxic. Also, L4's idea is the gender-swapped "saves you the last bite" line from when_a_boy_loves_you, so regular viewers may notice the reuse.

**8. Benchmark:** Closest to **girl_no_makeup** (a girl's quiet habit, the viewer is the boy, and a "if you ever point it out, it stops" turn). It **MATCHES**, line for line. It does not BEAT: the turn borrows makeup's turn and is slightly weaker than "watch how fast the makeup goes back on."

**9. Verdict: SHIP.** An optional trim to L6 (not counted as a fix): end it at "...she'll actually finish her plate."

## 7. boy_double_checks_the_door_is_locked_so_you_dont_have_to

**1. Line check**
- L1: He re-checks the lock after you, and it's not a dig at you. OK.
- L2: He gets up at midnight to check again quietly. OK, but uneasy.
- L3: Every night he does a lap of the stove, windows and car. OK, but it describes a compulsive ritual.
- L4: He does the lap even when you're not there. **FAIL (contradiction).** If you're not there, it isn't "so you don't have to." It also confirms the behaviour is compulsive.
- L5: Send to him. OK.
- L6: Don't tell him to stop, or you'll miss the sound of him checking. Risky (see the risk check).
- L7: He checks so you can stop worrying. OK.

**2. Hook check:** Pass.

**3. Beat check:** Pass on concreteness. What the beats show, together, is an anxiety ritual.

**4. Send check:** Pass.

**5. Turn check:** Structurally a pass, but it tells the partner not to question the behaviour.

**6. Closer check:** Pass.

**7. Risk check:** **Medium.** Repeated night-time checking laps read as OCD or anxiety, and L6 frames gently addressing that as a loss. It romanticises a compulsion. There's no surveillance element.

**8. Benchmark:** Closest to **girl_no_makeup** (turn shape). It **FALLS SHORT**: L4 breaks the premise.

**9. Verdict: CUT.** L3, L4 and L6 all need replacing, and the premise itself carries the risk.
