# Round 1 script review: 7 Nov 2026 drafts (10 scripts)

I scored every script as written. Where I give fixes, I also give a projected score with the fixes in. I edited no files.

Only 2 of the 10 clear the approval bar, and both only with fixes. Nothing ships as written.

## Problems that run through the whole round

**A. Every line 1 uses the same formula.** Each one is "When a [boy/girl] does X, [he/she]'s not Y." That runs 20–28 words, so about 6–8 seconds of voice-over before the picture lands. The opening line of all five winners is a short scene with no "he's not being X" disclaimer:
- "He fights sleep for ten minutes..."
- "Pyjamas, spot cream..."
- "Washed and folded is the scariest way..."

The disclaimer also gives the reframe away in second 3. I've written a winner-style replacement hook for every script that survives.

**B. Six of the ten line 6s are the same template.** All of families a, b and c use "But the day she stops X... she's not finally Y. She's stopped Z." Posted in the same fortnight, viewers will notice the pattern. Do not post more than one of these in the same week.

**C. Both "mirror" scripts (family e) have no warning line.** Line 6 is meant to be a warning with a turn. In both e scripts it is just an observation, so the format is broken.

## 1. girl_heating_too_high (family a)

**Line check**
- L1: She turns the heating up when she's cold, and isn't doing it to bother you. PASS, but it's long, and "even mid-conversation" is filler.
- L2: She turns it up three times and denies it. PASS, and it's the best line in the script.
- L3: She keeps your hoodie by the thermostat "for this moment." **FAIL. Contradiction:** if she has the hoodie, why is she turning up the heat? And "this moment" has nothing to point to.
  - Replacement: "And when you turn it back down, she just puts your hoodie on and turns it up again."
- L4: "She'd rather sweat it out together than ask you to leave and grab her a blanket." **FAIL. Riddle:** leave where? Who is sweating, and why?
  - Replacement: "She'd rather you both sit in a sauna than sit on the other sofa."
- L5: If you're the one always taking your jumper off, send this to her. PASS.
- L6: When she stops reaching for the thermostat, she's stopped expecting you to notice. **FAIL. Doesn't follow:** she turns the thermostat herself, and no beat sets up that she wants you to notice anything.
  - Replacement: "But the night she leaves the heating alone and sits on the other end of the sofa... she's not finally warm. She's stopped wanting to be close enough to argue about it."
- L7: She wants you "close enough to complain about it together." **FAIL.** Complain about what? It's a fortune cookie.
  - Replacement: "Because a girl who fights you for the thermostat... just wants the room warm enough that you'll stay in it with her."

**Hook:** Thermostat wars are recognisable. But the territory sits right next to girl_cold_feet, and the account has a history of near-clones flopping. Avoid any "She says she's not cold. The thermostat says 26" hook: that is the same shape as the plate flop.
- Better line 1: "She turns the heating up. You turn it down. Neither of you has said a word about it in three years."

**Send:** Yes. A man would send it to her ("this is you"). It's funny and low-stakes.

**Twist:** Not a real turn as written. The fixed version works.

**Risk:** None.

**Scores:** Reach 6, Engagement 5, Relatability 7 (avg 6.0), P(1k) 22%. With fixes: 6/6/7 (avg 6.3), P(1k) 28%.

**Verdict: CUT this round.** It's still under the bar even after fixes, and it's too close to cold_feet. Bench the fixed version for a week when the feed has no temperature or hoodie posts.

## Problems in every script

**A. Line 1 is too long.**
- 9 of 10 openers run 20-28 words, and 9 of 10 scripts lean on "every single time" or "every single night" padding.
- The winners open with 12-18 words of picture ("Washed and folded is the scariest way to get your hoodie back").
- The drafts did drop the "When a boy does X, he's not Y" disclaimer, which is good. But they swapped the disclaimer for padding, so the setup is just as slow.

**B. Line 6 is the banned slot in disguise.**
- Seven scripts use "he's/she's not finally X. He's/she's stopped Y", and the bed script uses "that's not nothing. That's…". Restaurant and tired use a "you're the reason" turn instead.
- In at least four of them, X and Y are synonyms or unrelated.
- This is the same mechanical fill that produced "that's not distance, that's hesitation."

**C. Family (a)/(b)/(c) warnings all read as "watch for signs they've stopped loving you."** That teaches viewers to monitor a partner for withdrawal, next door to the "check his phone" genre, and is flagged as risk.

**D. Line 7 mostly restates line 1 with "Because a [boy/girl] who…". It rarely adds anything.**

**E. Point of view is muddled in the mirror scripts.** "She" and "he" switch to "you" mid-script.
