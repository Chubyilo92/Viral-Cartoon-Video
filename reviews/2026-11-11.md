# Round 1 — 2026-11-11 gap-fill run

Reviewer: independent and harsh. I did not write these drafts. The source is /tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/0249ff95-995a-5583-b58d-f92b8b1ee8f3/scratchpad/drafts-2026-11-11-round1.md (10 scripts, 7 lines each). I benchmarked them against the 5 real winners as given in the brief (fidelity, when_a_boy_loves_you, girl_steals_hoodie, girl_no_makeup, trust).

How I applied the verdict rule: a script gets a benchmark judgement as written, and a second one with the listed fixes applied. SHIP WITH FIXES is only allowed when the fixed version MATCHES or BEATS its benchmark **and** the fixes touch two lines or fewer. If a script needs three or more replacement lines, that is a rewrite, and it is CUT.

All 10 drafts pass three checks by template: a "send this to" line, a turn that starts with "But", and a closer that starts with "Because". So the real differences are in whether the lines make sense, how concrete the beats are, how real the turn is, and the risk.

## 1. girl_still_brags_about_you_to_strangers

**Line check**
1. Girl brags about you to strangers, not to show off. PASS.
2. Tells the hairdresser you fixed her car, early in the appointment. PASS, just. "Before her hair's even wet" is a compressed image. It works, but a caption reader needs half a second to get it. Optional: "She tells the hairdresser you fixed her car before she's even sat down."
3. Tells her mum on speakerphone so the shop hears. **FAIL.** Her mum is not a stranger. The beat leaves the behaviour named in the hook and turns into "she tells her mum", which is ordinary.
   Replacement: **"She tells the taxi driver how you two met, and he never even asked."**
4. Tells the barista your name before her own order. PASS. Slightly odd but clear and visual.

**Hook:** Broad subject ("a girl") plus one concrete behaviour ("brags about you to total strangers") plus a "not X" negation in the shape of hoodie and no_makeup. PASS.
**Beats:** Lines 2 and 4 are one-picture actions of the same behaviour. Line 3 drifts off it (fixed above).
**Send:** "If she talks about you like you're the best thing that happened to her... send this to her." PASS. Clear condition, specific recipient.
**Turn:** "But if you ever roll your eyes when she does it... she'll learn to stop telling people about you." This is a real consequence aimed at the viewer, and it has the same shape as girl_no_makeup's "make a joke... watch how fast the makeup goes back on." PASS.
**Closer:** "Because a girl who brags about you to strangers... wants the whole world to know she picked well." It stands alone and is quotable. "She picked well" is a good twist because it is about her pride, not his ego. PASS.
**Risk:** None.
**Benchmark:** **girl_no_makeup** (same skeleton: the behaviour stops if the viewer mocks it). As written: FALLS SHORT because line 3 breaks the behaviour. With the fix: **MATCHES**. The turn is as real as no_makeup's. The beats are a little less intimate than "spot cream on her chin", but they are equally concrete.
**Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES.** Line 3 becomes "She tells the taxi driver how you two met, and he never even asked."

## 2. boy_remembers_your_familys_birthdays_better_than_you_do

**Line check**
1. He remembers your family's birthdays better than you, and it's more than being organised. PASS.
2. Texts your mum happy birthday before you're awake. PASS. Strong.
3. Asks about your brother's exam results weeks later. **FAIL (off-behaviour).** Exam results are not a birthday. This is a separate claim ("he remembers family things"), which makes the beats a chain of claims instead of one behaviour.
   Replacement: **"He knows your dad's birthday is the 14th without checking his phone."**
4. Your nan's birthday is in his calendar with its own alarm. PASS. Strong and specific.

**Hook:** The subject is broad and the behaviour is concrete. "Better than you do" adds a gentle sting. PASS.
**Beats:** Lines 2 and 4 are excellent. Line 3 drifts (fixed above).
**Send:** "If he keeps track of your family like they're his own... send this to him." PASS.
**Turn:** "But if you forget his mum's birthday two years running... he notices exactly what that means." **FAIL.** "Exactly what that means" is a riddle, because it never says what it means. It also reads as scorekeeping and guilt-tripping the viewer. The no_makeup and trust turns also hit the viewer, but they name a plain consequence ("the makeup goes back on", "one more reason she can't trust").
   Replacement: **"But if he's the only one who ever remembers... one day he'll stop reminding you."**
**Closer:** "Because a boy who remembers your family's birthdays... is building a life with all of you, not just you." It is quotable and the idea is strong. It runs a bit long, but it is acceptable. PASS.
**Risk:** The original turn edges toward "keep score in a relationship". The replacement removes that.
**Benchmark:** **girl_no_makeup** (the viewer-action turn). As written: FALLS SHORT, because the turn is vague and one beat drifts. With the fixes: **MATCHES**. Lines 2 and 4 are as concrete as anything in the top 5.
**Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES.** Line 3 and line 6 replaced as above. This is the limit: two fixes.

## 3. girl_hides_your_old_tshirt_so_you_cant_throw_it_out

**Line check**
1. She hides your old t-shirt, and she's "not being sentimental for no reason". **FAIL.** The negation backfires: it admits she *is* sentimental and only argues about the reason. Hoodie's "she's not just cold" negates the obvious explanation. This one doesn't.
   Replacement: "When a girl hides your old t-shirt so you can't throw it out, she's not just saving a shirt."
2. Moves it to the back of the drawer when you mention a clear-out. PASS.
3. Wears it to bed when you're away, holes and all. PASS, but it is a near copy of hoodie line 2.
4. Swears she's never seen it while it's folded under her side. PASS, but the syntax is clunky and it is a near copy of hoodie line 3 ("she'll swear she's giving it back").

**Hook:** Concrete, but long (19 words before the comma clause) and the negation is weak.
**Beats:** Concrete and on-behaviour, but every one is a hoodie beat in a different shirt.
**Send:** "If there's a t-shirt of yours that's mysteriously still around... send this to her." PASS.
**Turn:** "But the day she finally lets you bin it... she's not decluttering. She's letting go of something else too." Structurally this is hoodie's turn, but "something else too" is vaguer than hoodie's blunt "She's saying goodbye." It is a weaker copy of the original's best line.
**Closer:** "...isn't ready to let go of any version of you." Decent, but softer than "is keeping you close".
**Risk:** No content risk. There is a strategic risk: the account would be posting a visibly weaker clone of its own #3, and followers will notice.
**Benchmark:** **girl_steals_hoodie**. **FALLS SHORT** line for line. The hook negation is weaker, the beats are copies, the turn is vaguer and the closer is softer. Nothing in it beats the original.
**Verdict: CUT.**

## 4. boy_keeps_a_notes_app_list_of_your_inside_jokes

**Line check**
1. He keeps a list of your inside jokes, and it's "not just forgetful". **FAIL.** Nobody watching assumes "forgetful". The negation knocks down an explanation no one holds.
   Replacement: "When a boy keeps a notes app list of your inside jokes, he's not just taking notes."
2. Adds one at 2am after a message makes him laugh "on his own, in bed, alone". **FAIL.** "On his own" and "alone" say the same thing, and it is unclear whose message it is.
   Replacement: "He adds a new one at 2am because something you texted made him laugh out loud."
3. Reads one at dinner and only you two laugh. PASS. This is the best beat.
4. The first one dates from before you were official. PASS.

**Hook:** Concrete behaviour, broken negation.
**Beats:** Lines 3 and 4 are good. Line 2 is muddled.
**Send:** "If you've ever caught him smiling at his phone for no reason... send this to him." **Weak.** The condition has nothing to do with the list. "Smiling at his phone for no reason" is also the classic set-up for a cheating suspicion, which is the opposite of the intended feeling.
**Turn:** "But if he deletes the list after a fight and says it 'didn't mean anything'... it meant everything, and he's hiding how much." **FAIL, and a risk.** This is not a consequence. It tells the viewer to read a hurtful, punishing act after a fight as secret love. The top 5 do the opposite: when_a_boy_loves_you calls the red flag a red flag ("maybe he's not the one").
**Closer:** "...is quietly building a whole language just for the two of you." Strong and quotable. It is the best closer of the round, and it is wasted here.
**Risk: YES.** The turn romanticises emotional withholding and cruelty after a fight ("he said it meant nothing, but he really loves you").
**Benchmark:** **when_a_boy_loves_you**. **FALLS SHORT.** Its turn turns that script's moral upside down.
**Verdict: CUT** (it needs four replacement lines and has a risk flag). The concept and closer are worth bringing back in a later round with a turn like "But if you ever laugh at him for keeping it... the list stops growing."

## 5. boy_always_lets_you_have_the_closer_parking_spot

**Line check**
1. He takes the far spot so you get the one by the door. **FAIL (premise).** This only works if you arrive in two separate cars at the same place at the same time, which is a niche situation. Most viewers will picture one car and the logic falls apart. The slug ("lets you have") and the hook ("takes the far spot") also describe it differently.
2. Circles twice looking for "it", then gives up and parks further out "himself". **FAIL (riddle).** Looking for what, for whom? If he gave up, where did you park?
3. Walks the extra two minutes back without mentioning it. PASS, if you accept two cars.
4. Does it even with his arms full of your shopping bags. **FAIL (contradiction).** If you came in separate cars, why is he carrying your shopping from his far spot?

**Hook / Beats:** The premise is incoherent, so the beats can't land.
**Send:** "If you've never once had to walk far to get to the car... send this to him." PASS on its own.
**Turn:** "...he's not in a rush. He's stopped thinking about you first." The structure is fine.
**Closer:** "...puts your five minutes ahead of his own." Decent.
**Risk:** None.
**Benchmark:** **when_a_boy_loves_you** (a small sacrifice, like "saves you the last bite"). **FALLS SHORT.** The behaviour itself doesn't hold together. The real behaviour is "drops you at the door when it's raining", which would be a different script.
**Verdict: CUT.**

## 6. girl_orders_you_a_water_without_asking

**Line check**
1. Orders you water, "not trying to control what you drink". **FAIL (risk framing).** The hook brings up "control" itself, and in a drinks context it suggests he drinks too much and she manages it.
   Replacement: "When a girl orders you a water without asking, she's not just being polite."
2. Slides it over before the waiter has finished taking the order. **FAIL (contradiction).** The water can't be on the table before the order is even taken.
3. Reminds you to drink it halfway through. Borderline. It reads as mild nagging.
4. Orders you one "even when you didn't come out together". **FAIL (riddle).** If you're not out together, how is she ordering for you?

**Hook:** Concrete behaviour, bad negation.
**Beats:** Two of the three are logically broken.
**Send:** "If there's always a glass of water near you that you didn't order... send this to her." PASS.
**Turn:** "But the night you finally remember it yourself... she notices. It's one less thing she has to carry." **FAIL.** That is a happy outcome, not a turn. It also recasts her care as a burden she carries, which undercuts the whole script.
**Closer:** "...is looking after you in the ways you don't even notice." Generic.
**Risk: Mild.** It frames her as managing his drinking ("control" is in the very first line).
**Benchmark:** **when_a_boy_loves_you** (small ways of caring). **FALLS SHORT** on every check except send.
**Verdict: CUT.**

## 8. girl_turns_the_volume_down_the_instant_your_phone_rings

**Line check**
1. She turns the volume down when your phone rings, and it's more than being considerate. Weak. "Considerate" is exactly what it is, so the negation has nothing to push against.
2. Mutes the show mid-sentence, even her favourite episode. PASS.
3. Shushes everyone before you've said hello. PASS.
4. Pauses a film without complaining about losing her place. **FAIL (duplicate).** This is line 2 again with "pause" instead of "mute".
   Replacement: "She'll sit through the whole call in silence, then hand you the remote."
**Send:** "If she goes quiet the second your phone rings, every single time... send this to her." PASS, but see the risk below.
**Turn:** "...it's not that she stopped caring about the noise. She stopped caring who was calling." **Ambiguous.** It's meant as "she stopped caring about you", but "who was calling" points at the caller, which suggests she was monitoring your calls.
**Closer:** "...makes sure you're never competing with the TV for her attention." **FAIL (logic inverted).** She turns the TV down for your *call*. It's the caller who would compete with the TV, and her attention isn't on your call, or shouldn't be.
   Replacement: "Because a girl who turns the volume down for your calls... puts your world ahead of hers, even for a minute."
**Risk: YES, moderate.** "Goes quiet the second your phone rings, every single time" plus "who was calling" reads to a lot of viewers as a girlfriend listening in on your calls. This is the jealousy and surveillance reading that the fidelity audience would be primed to notice.
**Benchmark:** **girl_steals_hoodie** (the future-day turn). **FALLS SHORT.** The closer contradicts the behaviour, a beat is duplicated, and the turn can be read as surveillance.
**Verdict: CUT** (three replacements needed plus a risk flag).
